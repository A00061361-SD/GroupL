## Introduction

Over the past two decades, social media has become an incredibly important and relevant factor in everyday life. It has completely changed the way we communicate, share, and connect with our friends and family across the world. 
Social networking sites began as online platforms that allowed anybody to create a personal profile of themselves and to communicate with their friends and explore their own and other users' profiles (Boyd and Ellison, 2007).
Over time, social media transformed into something much bigger than just profile-based networking, it started to focus a lot more on visual content, such as short-form videos and personalized recommendations (Boyd and Ellison, 2007; Bhandari and Bimo, 2022).
It's pretty safe to say that social media has impacted so many different aspects of our current everyday life. Throughout our report, we will be exploring the evolution of social media platforms such as Myspace, Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok and investigating them from both a business and societal perspective.
We will also be looking into the ethical challenges that social media faces every day and the potential improvements that can be made in the future.

## Historical Development

Social Networking can be traced all the way back to 1997 with the launch of a website called “SixDegrees.com”. It was the first of its kind back when it was launched.
It allowed anybody who had access to the internet to create a personal profile and list their friends (Boyd and Ellison, 2007). Although it was a very limited website, it paved the way for future social networking platforms.
Six years after the creation of “SixDegrees.com” another social network emerged called “Myspace”. Unlike any platform that came before, it offered much more in-depth customisation and freedom when it came to personalising your personal profile, 
becoming incredibly popular among teenagers and musicians (Dhingra and Mudgal, 2019). 
Fast forward a year later, Mark Zuckerberg, Eduardo Saverin, Dustin Moskovitz, Chris Hughes, and Andrew McCollum created and launched a social media platform called “Facebook”.
The platform was originally launched to connect students studying at Harvard University, but because of its popularity among the students, they expanded it to other universities and eventually it was available worldwide by 2006 to anyone over the age of 13 (Dhingra and Mudgal, 2019).
By 2008 it had completely overtaken Myspace in popularity and was the main social network used worldwide (Penni, 2015).

Not long after Facebook’s worldwide launch, a social media platform released in 2010 under the name “Instagram”. 
Kevin Systrom and Mike Krieger, the creators, had a slightly different approach when it came to their social network. They focused heavily on visual content, through pictures and eventually short videos, rather than the friendships, written updates and personal profiles of Facebook.
It was a huge success, and the platform was eventually bought out and acquired by Facebook in 2012 (Fuciu, 2019).
Jumping ahead to 2018, Musical.ly and TikTok merged into a single platform. Not long after the merger, the platform blew up in popularity and was rapidly becoming one of the most popular platforms on the internet.
In the first three months of 2020 alone, the platform received 315 million downloads (Bhandari and Bimo, 2022). TikTok’s focus was and still is short-form video content. The reason it stood out is mainly due to its incredibly in-depth personalised algorithm through its “for you” page, 
which would display content tailored specifically for each individual user's interests (Bhandari and Bimo, 2022).

## Business viewpoint
Social media plays an important role across different aspects of modern businesses nowadays, with brands using platforms for various things such as marketing, advertising, and customer service. This is due to the cost-effective access to large audiences that can be reached with targeted content. Social media is now being used to increase sales by building relationships with customers through actively monitoring and responding to customer feedback, maintaining a positive brand image. Businesses are becoming increasingly aware that sharing compelling content can encourage users to click through and explore a business’ offering which can lead to increased website visits, conversions, and increase revenue. 

The increasing use of social media has created data in the form of likes, comments, shares, and clicks on platforms that businesses analyze to obtain an insight into customer behavior and preferences. This provides a deeper understanding of target audiences and can be used to tailor marketing efforts and create more engagement. More businesses are viewing influencers as an asset and are investing their time in partnerships to reach a wider audience, with the hope of bringing in new customers and creating more interest in their brand overall. While partnering with a widely known influencer would seem more beneficial, many businesses are focusing their energy on partnering with influencers with a following of people that would be interested in what they have to offer. For example, Joshi (2024) states that his New York City-based company would benefit more from someone with 100,000 followers living in New York City rather than someone with 10 million followers spread across the globe. Forbes (2024). The Power of Social Media in Modern Marketing. Available at: https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesbusinesscouncil/2024/10/09/the-power-of-social-media-in-modern-marketing/ 

 

Prior to social media, businesses promoted themselves through television, radio, newspapers, billboards, and magazines. Without the ability to reach large audiences in a small amount of time, campaigns needed to be carefully planned because of the opportunity to reach audiences being limited. This pushed brands to focus on advertisements leaving a strong and lasting impression using memorable slogans, emotional storytelling, and recognizable identity. Before social media trends became a tool to create brand growth, one of the strongest tools was word-of-mouth. If the product was good, customers would share their experience with friends, family, and communities. 

The era before social media prompted brands to work harder to earn attention, rather than chasing it. This created stronger customer loyalty because businesses were focusing on a customer base that had already been established and brought in income. Older brands had developed an understanding that product quality was the foundation of long-term success, as their survival depended on customer satisfaction and reputation. Hamza (2026). Without social media to capture attention, businesses had to put greater effort into the quality of products, as well as their advertising strategies, and maintain a positive relationship with customers. Think Marketing Magazine (2026). Marketing Before Social Media: Why It Worked. Available at: 

https://thinkmarketingmagazine.com/marketing-before-social-media-why-it-worked/ 

 

The arrival of marketing through social media allowed businesses to grow rapidly, but how did it start? The earliest examples of these dates back to the mid-2000s, with the arrival of platforms such as MySpace and Facebook. Businesses began creating pages to shar updates and connect with their customers. The early strategies relied on likes, comments and shares to build brand visibility. Content was designed to engage users without paid promotion through informative or entertaining advertisements. As the number of people using these platforms grew, they became a key part of digital marketing plans. The development of paid advertising features allowed businesses to expand their reach and target users with greater accuracy. For example, the launch of Facebook Ads in 2007 allowed businesses to run targeted campaigns using user demographics, interests, and behavior. Dejnak (2025). As competition grew, paid promotions became essential for staying visible and reaching larger audiences. 

Over the past twenty years, social media marketing has changed drastically, evolving from basic content sharing to a strategy rich discipline that incorporates advanced technology, diverse engagement methods and influencers to create income for a business. Today, social media marketing has advanced significantly, including dynamic ads, A/B testing, and predictive analytics. These tools help brands personalize content and improve performance in real time. Platforms offer interactive features, such as live streaming, polls, and AR filters, that increase user engagement and time spent on content. An example of this advancement can be seen in Sephora’s approach to marketing. The company uses AR for virtual try-ons, allowing users to test makeup through their phones. This blend of e-commerce and interactivity shows how social media has become a complex system combining creativity, data, and real-time engagement. Landingi (2025). Social Media Marketing History and Its Revolution Over Decades. Available at: https://landingi.com/social-media-marketing/history/

## Ethical reflection and improvements
Privacy:

Privacy is an important aspect of consumer technology as people do not want others seeing sensitive information like passwords ,emails or real government information like real names or surnames and conversations
they have with others. Consumers today heavily base what applications they will use on the strength of the privacy protection that apps or websites offer. Consumers now are aware that their data is 
being collected and question how their data will be used. 

Problems with data collection:

There are laws about data protection in over 150 countries but most consumers still have concerns about privacy protection especially when data breaches happen.
A big data breach happened in 2014. The Cambridge Analytica Scandal which happened through Facebook (Meta). Where 87 million people had there data stolen by bad actors, Cambridge Analytica filed for bankruptcy
and Facebook(Meta) was fined $5billion in 2019 for privacy violations. However bad actors are not the only people who consumers are concerned with when it comes privacy protection, some companies have been known to misuse user data for 
profit by selling it to add corporations without disclosing that the users data would be used in such a way. Meta is known to often breach the trust of their consumers by mishandling consumer data. In 2023 Meta was fined 
€1.2 billion by EU regulators for mishandling the transfer of user data between europe and the united states, Meta failed to adress surveilance risks identified in the court of justice of the european union.

Targeted algorithms and advertisements:

Another big problem consumers face with most of these apps like Instagram, tiktok etc. Is the data collection for algorithm or advertisement purposes. These apps collect data to push targeted content onto consumers.
This has big problems as most consumers have lost their sense of privacy as these companies collect your data most of the time without consent. You see this on many websites that use "cookies" there
is no ovbious option to opt out of these cookies and only provide the options of "limit" or "only neccessary". This is something that needs to be adressed by law makers as this data that is being collected with or without
conset and is often breached, misused or mishandling.

Online safety:

There is a serious lack of online safety provided to consumers. Prolems like online exploitation, insufficent regulations, harmful algorithms etc. Effect people in negative ways but the worst effect of this is that 
it effects children. Children who have access to the internet and apps like Instagram, TikTok or games like roblox are often subjected to online exploitation by predators. However this doesn't only effect children,
the elderly are also more at risk of being effected by the lack of online safety or transparency from companies. For example elderly people are more likely to fall for phishing links sent to their emails or something
more simple like not being able to read small text on a website and agreeing to bad terms. This can be addressed by improving digital literacy. For example we could teach students about online safety and potentially how to 
identify risks and exploiters or strengthening cyber security and improving privacy laws to prevent companies from exploiting elderly people.

Government improvements:

Governments need to implement policies that will limit companies from collecting too much unnecessary data and will actually hold these companies accountable for protecting their consumers data and hand out appropriate punishments for failure to
comply with these policies. Companies should also be forced to be 100% transparent with how user data will be used and what exactly is being collected. As mentioned before governments could introduce digital literacy courses for all people to
maybe help people identify risks. Another area that that has not been mentioned yet is account creation. governments could introduce stronger age verification like document submissions to verify age and identity to prevent scammers or bad actors.
However this could have many problems like if there a data leak or a hack millions of people could have their identities stolen but not only that governments could use this data in harmful ways.
